# Brand and messaging

> Name, voice, visuals and answers to the questions outsiders ask.

Part of the [Product Brief Pack](index.md). The product's own brand is **mostly undecided**. What exists is a set of principles and a visual direction for the app.

## Brand and messaging

**In plain words:** The name **Band Studio OS is a working title**. There is no tagline, no logo and no approved messaging. What is settled is how the product should feel and sound: it belongs to the band's scene, it amplifies the band's own grit, and it never looks like corporate polish.

**Key facts**
- *Name:* working title only. Not decided.
- *Principles (Decided):* **authenticity first** (amplify a band's grit and subcultural identity, never sanitised polish) and **human in the loop** (the AI proposes, the band curates, nothing is published without approval).
- *Value line (Decided in the foundation):* "do more, do better, with less."
- *Tone of voice, draft:* plain, direct, a bit rough, on the band's side. Talks like a bandmate who has done this before, not like a software company. No hype words, no "supercharge", no "unlock". This is a proposed guide for Ale to confirm; no tone guide has been approved.
- *Draft key messages, derived only from decided positioning (not yet approved or tested with anyone):*
  - For a **band** (the driver): "Your next gig, promoted in one sitting, and it still looks like you."
  - For **bandmates**: "One tap to say yes, or tell us what's wrong."
  - For **venues and partners**: "Bands arrive with material that looks like them and is ready on time."
  - For **investors**: the creative and identity layer for independent bands' gig promotion; reach comes later through partners.
- *Boilerplate "about" text:* see the [Fact sheet](partners-and-press.md#fact-sheet) for a first short version.
- *Not yet known:* final name, tagline, approved key messages, tone guide, who the brand speaks to first in public.

**How sure are we:** principles *Decided*; everything else *Not yet known* or draft. No audience has seen any wording.

## Visual assets

**In plain words:** The app has a visual direction but **no brand identity yet**: no logo, no final name, no product colours, no screenshots of real output and no video.

**What exists**
- **The look of the app ("the band's wall"):** concrete grey, black ink, yellow tape, photocopy grain. Archivo for headlines and Courier Prime for diary-style detail. Deliberate imperfection, because real gig posters are photocopied. No gradients, glass or emoji. The app owns no colour: the band's artwork supplies it.
- **A visual board** of the direction and a board comparing three journey designs, both viewable in a browser.
- **Clickable phone prototypes** (second version, 2.1 with draft later journeys, and 2.2, the version built for test sessions with a pitch page, onboarding and the Identity Studio).
- Sample posters inside the prototypes are **drawn by code with canned content**. They are not output of the real product and must not be shown as such.

**What does not exist:** logo, product colours and fonts as a brand (the fonts above are the app's), screenshots of real generated output, a product video, a demo recording.

## FAQ and objections

Answers are written to be honest. Where we do not know, the answer says so.

**Why not just use Canva?** Canva is good, free to start, and many bands make fine flyers in it. What we aim to add is one flow for the whole gig (flyer, stories, post, captions) in your band's own voice, with your bandmates approving from their phones. Whether that is better than Canva for a band is exactly what we have not yet tested. *Not yet shown.*

**Isn't AI-made art generic, or "slop"?** It can be, and some venues refuse AI-made posters (we found examples in the US and Canada, none in Italy or the UK). That is why the product starts from the band's identity, lets you say what you never want to look like, and never publishes without your band's approval. Whether the output really feels like a band is the first thing we test. *Hypothesis.*

**Will it post for us?** No. In the first version you download a ready-to-post folder and post it yourself. *Decided.*

**Do my bandmates need an account?** No. They get a link and approve or veto from their phones. *Decided as the plan; not tested.*

**Does it work for my genre?** There is no genre limit; each band sets its own style. Indie and rock are the reference scene. *Decided; not tested outside indie and rock.*

**What does it cost?** The first pilot is free. Prices are not set. *Not yet known.*

**Who owns the images it makes?** *Not yet known.* We have not reviewed ownership, music copyright or image rights with a lawyer. The first version avoids uploads of gig photos and audio to keep this risk small.

**What happens to our data?** *Not yet reviewed.* EU privacy rules apply; no raw media is sent to analytics. The details are decided in the architecture phase.

**Is it only for bands that are already doing well?** No: it is meant for small bands with little time and money. Money is, however, the main barrier independent artists name, so price will matter a lot. *Hypothesis.*

**How sure are we:** the answers repeat what is decided; most claims they lean on are untested.
