# Market and competition

> How big the opportunity might be and who else is in it.

Part of the [Product Brief Pack](index.md). Read together with [Evidence and risks](evidence-and-risks.md), because every number here is a range built on assumptions.

## Market

**In plain words:** We cannot yet say how big this market is. Desk research bounds it very loosely: yearly revenue from Italy and the UK together could be anywhere between about **16 thousand and 6.4 million euros**, with a middle ("base") case of about **446 thousand euros**. That is a 400-fold spread, because the three assumptions that drive it have no solid source. "Not known yet" is the answer, not "too small".

**Key facts**
- **Bands:** about 74 thousand active, in-segment bands in Italy and the UK (range 29 thousand to 178 thousand). The UK number is the best sourced (a venue trade body counted nearly 1.5 million artist performances at its 810 member venues in 2024); the Italian number rests on 2016 data and assumptions (range about 3 thousand to 47 thousand). *Needs primary research.*
- **Who would pay:** assumed 3% to 15% of bands that run promotion digitally, base 8%. No solid source exists. One vendor survey says 75% of musicians who pay for promotion spend more than they earn from music. *Hypothesis.*
- **Price per paying band:** assumed 60 to 300 euros a year, base 150. The only benchmark is a competitor that charges about 2 euros per contact. *Hypothesis.*
- **What the income ladder says (base price, no minimum set):** about 200 paying bands give 30 thousand euros a year; about 670 give 100 thousand. Ale chose not to set a minimum market size up front.
- **Wider Europe:** a further 2 to 6 times Italy plus UK, base 4 times (assumption).
- **Spend check from the other direction:** all in-segment bands together spend perhaps 6 to 30 million euros a year on promotion in the UK and 0.1 to 3 million in Italy, again on assumed shares. The two methods agree within about 1.1 times at the base case, but they share the same unsourced assumptions, so that agreement proves little.
- **Trend:** mixed to negative at the grassroots. UK grassroots shows fell 8.3% since 2023 and a venue closed every fortnight in 2024; Italian small clubs are in difficulty even while stadium revenue sets records. Active bands in Italy rose while weekly concerts stayed flat (2016 data), so each band gets fewer gigs. No clean growth series exists.
- **Why now:** *not yet known* as a story. What we can say is that AI tools for brand-aware campaigns have just arrived for general businesses, which makes the timing both an opening and a threat.

**How sure are we:** *Needs primary research.* No kill criterion triggered; the evidence is simply too thin.

## Competition

**In plain words:** Nothing we found does the whole job. The job is split across design tools, schedulers and caption tools, music-marketing add-ons, and doing it by hand. A band stitches these together. But **the generic version of our flow already exists** for small businesses, and the closest tools are moving toward us.

**The landscape in one view**

| Kind | Examples | What they do | Our view |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Design tools | Canva, PosterMyWall, Adobe Express | Templates, brand kits, resizing, AI features. Canva is free to start and about 13 to 18 USD a month for Pro | The main rival: "good enough" for most bands, known by everyone |
| AI campaign tools for small businesses | Simplified, Canva's newer AI features | A brief in, a set of posts out, in a saved brand kit, with approval before publishing. Simplified Pro is about 19 USD a month billed yearly, with extra costs for AI use and scheduling | The closest copy of our flow, with no band focus and no independent test for bands |
| Music-specific tools | Mew Design (AI posters with story crops), Krumzi (gig flyers for DJs and musicians, about 49 USD a month pass) | Flyer-first, band or DJ framing | Show that a band layer can be built; nothing does the full campaign |
| Reach and promotion | Bandsintown Boost (June 2026, flat fee per show) | Gets the show seen. No creative | Complementary: an indirect competitor and a possible partner later |
| By hand | Friends, freelancers, free tools, group chats, doing nothing | Free in money, costly in time | What we replace |
| Dead products | Zankme, Magnifi, DEMAND, PureVolume (all shut down 2014 to 2020) | Music promotion platforms | A warning: small revenue from time- and cash-poor musicians is plausible, not proven |

**Where we win and lose.** On criteria we set before looking, Canva scores about 79 out of 100, PosterMyWall about 77 and our *planned* product about 77. We only get ahead on things not yet built: the guided gig campaign, time saved, Italian. Today Canva wins. Where we can differ: the band's identity layer (grit, "never like this"), the whole gig in one flow, and approval by bandmates.

**"Why not Canva?"** Honest answer for now: you can make a good flyer in Canva, and many bands do. What nobody has shown is a tool that takes one sentence about a gig and returns a full campaign that still looks like your band, which your bandmates can approve from their phones. Whether we deliver that is exactly what the planned blind test checks. Until then we say "not shown yet".

**Can others copy it?** Yes, in principle. Simplified or Canva could add band templates in a release. Nothing shows the band layer is hard to copy. That is why the position rests on the band's own identity and not on the generic flow.

**How sure are we:** the landscape is *Provisional*: desk research, vendor pages for features and prices, a few independent reviews, and some quotes from online forums that Ale accepted as confirmed without our being able to open them. Canva's own pages could not be opened. Whether bands would switch is *Needs primary research*.
