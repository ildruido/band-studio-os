# R-002 Band count size check: report

Protocol: [protocol.md](protocol.md), pre-registered in commit 9a2cb1a on 2026-11-04. Tier: light (self-review). Method: `docs/research/framework.md`.

## Verdict

**Needs primary research.** We found no head count of active emerging bands for Italy or the UK, and no checkable derivation that reaches the thresholds. The numbers we could open show a large pool of Italian music rights holders, but not how many of them are in active bands. Nothing contradicts the thresholds either. H1 stays **Insufficient evidence** at Low confidence. This is a "not found", not a "no".

| Id | Hypothesis | Verdict | Confidence | Key evidence | Key evidence against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 Exists (size): at least 5,000 active bands in Italy, or at least 15,000 in Italy plus the UK | Insufficient evidence | Low | E-10 (112,000 SIAE members in 2023), E-08 (KeepOn 2016: 605 concerts a week; active bands up 28%) | none (but see the strongest case against) |

The R-001 flags on H1 and H3 are about missing evidence; this study does not change H3.

## Strongest evidence against our idea

1. **Nobody counts bands.** The 112,000 SIAE members (E-10) include composers, authors, publishers and mandated heirs across all repertoires, and many bands never register with SIAE at all. The figure cannot become a band count without assumptions we have no source for.
2. **A flat Italian live market.** KeepOn found that the number of active bands grew by over 28% while weekly concerts stayed at about 605 (E-08, 2016). If that held, supply of bands far exceeds the gigs available, which fits "many bands" but also a crowded, cash-poor market where few can pay for tools.
3. **Possible UK decline in activity.** Unverified leads say UK artists touring fell 74% since the pandemic and venues keep closing. That points to fewer active bands, not more.

## Findings

- **Italy, rights holders (grade A):** SIAE's 2023 report to Parliament lists 112,000 members ("associati e mandanti", 2022: 108,000) and 19,000 new applications in 2023 (E-10). This is the best figure for the size of the creator pool in Italy, across all repertoires.
- **Italy, live circuit (grade B, 2016, outside the window):** the KeepOn circuit counted about 605 concerts a week in its original-music clubs, and active bands up over 28% (E-08). It does not give a number of bands.
- **Derivation we tried, and why it fails:** if the thresholds hold, 5,000 bands of about four members means about 20,000 musicians, around one in five SIAE members. We have no source for what share of members play in active bands, so this is a plausibility check, not evidence. It shows the thresholds are not absurd against the size of the pool, nothing more.
- **UK:** no grade A or B head count was opened. Leads exist (about 46,500 professional musicians; the 2023 Musicians' Census, which reached 5,867 musicians as a survey, not a count).

## What we could not find

- Any count of active emerging bands in either country, by any body.
- A Eurostat or ISTAT count for musicians (ISCO 2652) in Italy; a data file exists but its figures were not reachable in this study.
- How many of SIAE's members play in bands, and how many bands skip SIAE.
This is a search and access gap; it does not show that the group is small.

## Who is missing and other limits

Unregistered bands, bands outside the rights-society system, inactive bands, and the UK entirely. Italian sources are older than the 2018 window in one case (E-08).

## What would change our mind

A grade A source or a clearly sourced derivation putting active bands in Italy below 1,000 (or Italy plus the UK below 5,000) would contradict H1; a source at or above the thresholds, plus an independent one, would support it.

## Next: primary research needed

- Ask the people who hold the data: KeepOn LIVE (live-club circuit), Rockit (its band database and PRO members) and SIAE's live-events department, for the number of distinct acts playing live per year. A short email or call settles H1 faster than more desk work.
- Fold a size question into the first band-driver interviews ("how many bands do you play with or know in your area?").
- If a cheap proxy is wanted: count distinct acts on a few cities' venue listings for one month (a small, checkable sample).
