```text
Research question: how well can an independent music band that promotes its own gigs get a complete, good-looking set of promotion material today using existing design and social tools?

The person doing the job is one member of a small, unsigned band (rock, indie or similar) with a day job, no design training and little money. For each upcoming gig they need: a flyer or poster, an Instagram/Facebook story (9:16), a square or 4:5 feed post, and written captions for the announcement and a reminder. They want the material to look and sound like their own band, not like a generic template, and they may repeat this for a new gig every few weeks.

Tools to assess: Canva, PosterMyWall, Adobe Express, Mew Design, Simplified. Add any other tool you find that bands or small event promoters actually use for this job (including AI poster and caption generators, social schedulers and free alternatives).

For each tool, report with evidence:
1. Can it produce the full set (poster, story, feed post, captions, scheduling) from one set of gig details, in one guided flow, or does the user need to combine separate features or separate tools? What is the realistic number of steps and time for a non-designer?
2. Can it save the band's identity (logo, colours, fonts, tone of voice) and reuse it automatically for the next gig? What limits apply on free and paid plans?
3. What does it cost for this job, per month and per campaign, on the cheapest plan that actually does it (state currency, country, date of the price, and what the free plan blocks)?
4. How good is the output for a band's own look? What do independent users, designers and reviewers say about AI-generated output being generic, off-brand or needing heavy manual fixing, and what do they say about it being good enough?
5. What do real users who tried the tool for music or event promotion say they liked, disliked or gave up on?

Also report: any evidence that bands, venues or promoters consider AI-generated or template-based posters a problem or an advantage; and anything these tools cannot do for this job that users complain about. Give equal attention to evidence that these tools already solve the job well and to evidence that they fall short.

Sources and format: prefer each tool's own help-centre and pricing pages (for features and prices only), independent tutorials, hands-on reviews, forum or community threads and user reviews from 2025 or later. For every claim give the source URL, publisher, date and a short quote. Mark clearly what is a vendor claim versus an independent user or reviewer statement, and what you could not find or open. Do not assume any particular answer. End with a table rating each tool from 0 (poor) to 3 (strong) on: time to finish, cost fit for a low-budget band, how guided the flow is, how well it keeps a band's identity across gigs, and coverage of poster plus story plus post plus captions plus scheduling, with a one-line reason per cell.
```
