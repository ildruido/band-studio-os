# R-001 Persona reality check: report

Protocol: [protocol.md](protocol.md), pre-registered in commit aab5ab7 on 2026-11-04. Tier: standard. Method: `docs/research/framework.md`.

## Verdict

**Needs primary research.** Desk research could not settle the key hypotheses. We found no grade A source for the size of the group in Italy or the UK (H1), and almost no first-hand voices from Italian bands. What we did open is mostly vendor surveys and a few journalism pieces, mainly from the UK and US. They are consistent with the idea (independent artists mostly promote themselves, and the work wears them down), but they cannot carry it. No source we opened contradicts H1 to H4 outright. Two kill criteria are triggered **as literally worded**, by absence of evidence and not by contrary evidence (H1 and H3, see the flags below). That needs Ale's ruling.

| Id | Hypothesis | Verdict | Confidence | Key evidence | Key evidence against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 Exists | Meaningful numbers of emerging bands in Italy / UK | Insufficient evidence | Low | none that carries weight (E-06 is 2017, counts individual players, and is context only) | none found; no head count either |
| H2 Does the job | Members promote the band themselves | Insufficient evidence | Low | E-02 (91% self-promote; vendor survey, UK and US, all musician types); E-09 (academic, five non-Italian countries: 69% spend more time promoting online than a few years ago) | none found; outsourcing exists but no source measures it |
| H3 Job hurts | Promotion costs real time, money or results, voiced unprompted | Insufficient evidence | Low | E-01, E-02, E-07, E-09 (23% spend over half their working time on non-musical tasks such as social media and fan communication) | E-04 (money is the biggest barrier); S-01 lead (promotion ranked fifth, unverified) |
| H4 Workarounds fall short | Same shortcomings recur with today's tools | Insufficient evidence | Low | none independent | none independent (only vendor pages) |
| H5 Angle matters | Authentic, human-made identity matters to them | Insufficient evidence | Low | E-03 | none found |
| H6 Would pay (signal) | They already pay for comparable help | Insufficient evidence | Low | E-02 (those who spend promoting mostly lose money) | none found |

**Kill-criteria flags (for Ale).**

- **H3:** the criterion says "pain appears mainly when prompted or in vendor material, or promotion ranks outside the top five". Everything we opened for H3 is survey-prompted or vendor-published, plus one academic survey (E-09) that is not about Italy or bands. As worded, it is **triggered**, but by an access gap (Reddit and Facebook could not be read), not by contrary evidence.
- **H1:** the second limb says "no credible source supports 5,000 in Italy and 15,000 in Italy plus the UK". No source gives any head count, so it is **triggered by absence**.

Under the framework, a triggered criterion forces Reshape or Stop unless Ale overrules it and logs why. I read both as "not found", not "contradicted", which is why the study verdict is Needs primary research, the nearest equivalent. **Ale decides** whether to log that as an override. Protocol weakness: both criteria mix "contradicted" with "not found", so they trigger on silence. Future protocols should word a kill criterion as evidence against, with a separate rule for "not found".

## Strongest evidence against our idea

1. **Promotion is not their biggest problem; money is.** The largest independent-artist survey we opened says financial constraints are "more than twice as likely to be chosen as the next biggest barrier" (E-04). An UK survey says 84% of independent UK artists cannot afford to tour (E-05). If promotion ranks below money, gigs and time, a promotion tool may be a nice-to-have.
2. **Little money to pay with.** About three in four self-releasing artists treat music as a side hustle (E-04), and those who spend on promotion mostly spend more than they earn (E-02). This weakens every pay-per-campaign assumption (D-008).
3. **A scene that is hostile to AI-made art.** A venue in the US bans AI-made flyers and tells bands to hire human artists instead (E-03). Our principle "authenticity first" fits the scene, but generated flyers may be exactly what these scenes reject. This is a product risk, not just a persona detail.
4. **The Italian scene is weakly documented.** The only Italian live-music numbers we opened are from 2016 and cover venues, not bands (E-08, outside the time window). We have no Italian evidence on who does band promotion.

## Findings

- **H1.** We found no head count of active emerging bands for Italy or the UK from any source we could open. Leads exist but are unverified (see "Unverified leads"). The only Italian item, E-06, is from 2017 and counts individual players in four Facebook groups (2,101 active users, likely overlapping), not bands. It gives zero weight to H1.
- **H2.** The 91% in E-02 comes from a vendor, covers all musician types (including solo and DJ acts, which the protocol excludes) in the UK and US, and says nothing about Italy. E-09 adds that most musicians in five other countries spend more time on online promotion than before. Together they suggest self-promotion is common; neither shows that the active member of a band does it.
- **H3.** Vendor surveys report heavy strain from promotion and social media (E-01: 86% report significant mental strain; 67% say social media harms their wellbeing). A qualitative thesis finds resistance and anxiety about self-promotion across ages (E-07). These are prompted, vendor-published or small. E-09 is the strongest source (academic, 1,198 musicians in Brazil, Chile, the Netherlands, Nigeria and South Korea): 23% spend over half their working time on non-musical tasks such as social media and fan communication. It is not Italian, UK or band-specific. These suggest the pain is real; they do not show where promotion ranks.
- **H4.** We found only vendor pages (Canva, CapCut, template sellers) and no independent voices on what is wrong with them, so no finding either way.
- **H5.** E-03 is one US venue owner's opinion against AI flyers. It supports a product risk, not a measured preference of bands. The wider backlash stories are unverified leads. Nothing about gritty versus polished, and nothing from Italy.
- **H6.** No direct evidence on what bands pay for promotion or design. E-02 suggests that those who spend rarely recover it.
- **Biggest problems in their own words.** We could not collect these. The only sample we opened ranks money first (E-04); the same page also says discovery tops artists' wish lists and that independence means carrying "marketing workload… yourself" (E-04), so promotion-adjacent pain is present too. Voices from forums remain to be gathered. No Italian-language source of any kind was opened.

## Segment candidates

The evidence does not yet split into segments; this is an honest gap.

- One split the evidence hints at, **Low** confidence: by age (younger and older artists both report discomfort with self-promotion, E-07). Nothing else.
- Splits we would **expect** but have no evidence for: gigging live bands against studio-first acts; city against province; one driver against shared effort. These are assumptions for the interviews.

## Anti-personas

- **Solo or DJ acts.** Excluded by the protocol; most of the surveys we opened mix them in (E-02), so their numbers need care.
- **Acts with a label or manager.** The 91% in E-02 explicitly excludes them, so they sit outside the promotion job.
- No evidence-based anti-persona beyond these yet.

## What we could not find

- A head count of emerging bands in Italy or the UK (only unverified leads).
- Any grade B voices from Italian bands. Reddit refuses automated fetches and Facebook groups are not readable here.
- Independent complaints about today's tools (Canva and similar).
- What bands pay for promotion or design in Italy.
Absence in this list means the search and the tools did not reach it, not that it does not exist.

## Who is missing and other limits

- Italian bands, almost entirely. Evidence is UK and US, English, vendor-heavy.
- Quiet bands that do little promotion or quit; the search for the failed found only general burnout surveys (E-01).
- Sources are from artist-services companies whose customers are active online, so they over-represent engaged and digital-first artists.
- Reddit and Facebook were unreachable. Tool note: the page-fetch tool is blocked here, so pages were opened with `curl` and read as plain text. Reddit returned nothing.

## What would change our mind

- H1: a grade A source (SIAE, ISTAT, FIMI, Musicians' Union or similar) giving an Italian head count under 1,000 would contradict; one above 5,000 would support.
- H3: a body of unprompted forum threads where promotion is absent from the top five problems would contradict.
- H5: Italian bands showing a clear preference for polished, professional output would contradict.

## Next: primary research needed

- **Interviews** with 8 to 10 Italian band drivers (and a few UK): who promotes, what hurts, what they use, what they pay. Settles H2 to H4 and H6 and gives real quotes.
- **A grade A size check**: request or read the SIAE and ISTAT annual figures, FIMI and live-club association reports; this can also be desk work once page fetch works for those hosts.
- **Fake-door test** for willingness to pay (H6) later, once there is a prototype.
- **Prototype test** of AI-made versus human-made flyers with bands (H5), given the backlash risk in E-03.
