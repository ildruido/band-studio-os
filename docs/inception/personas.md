# Personas: Band Studio OS (working title)

**Status: provisional.** Built from the evidence in [R-001](../research/R-001-persona-reality-check/report.md) and [R-002](../research/R-002-band-count-size-check/report.md), both "Needs primary research" (D-013). Most traits are assumptions until interviews with Italian band drivers (launch market order: Italy, then UK, then the rest of Europe, D-012). Decisions behind it: D-005, D-007, D-011.

Tags: **[E-nn]** evidence id from the reports, confidence Low unless stated. **(Assumption)** no evidence yet. Names, ages and quotes are illustrative.

## Primary persona: the Driver (speculative)

*Illustrative name: Marta, 29, guitarist and singer of a four-piece indie-rock band from a mid-sized Italian city.*

| Aspect | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Role | The one active member who runs the band's promotion alongside a day job (Assumption). Music is a side job, not a living [E-04]. |
| Goals | Fill the next gig; look like themselves, not generic (Assumption); stop losing evenings to flyers and captions (Assumption). |
| Pains | Promotion eats time that should go to music [E-09: 23% of surveyed musicians spend over half their working time on non-musical tasks such as social media]. Dislikes and dreads self-promotion on social media [E-07]. Spending on promotion often doesn't pay back [E-02]. |
| Current tools | Instagram and a free design app (Assumption; only vendor pages support design-tool use). Group chat for approvals (Assumption, from the starting idea). |
| Budget and time | Very little of both [E-04]. Pay-per-campaign willingness is unknown (Assumption, D-008 not yet tested). |
| What would make them pay | A complete gig campaign in a fraction of the time that still looks like the band (Assumption). |
| Authenticity | Cares about a human, DIY feel (Assumption). One US venue owner rejects AI-made flyers [E-03]: a risk to design for, not proof of preference. |
| Quote (illustrative) | "I don't mind playing the gig. I mind the three evenings of posting before it." |

## Secondary persona: the Bandmate Reviewer (speculative)

| Aspect | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Role | Reviewer; not the buyer and not the daily user (D-005). |
| Goals | Make sure the band looks right; not spend time on it (Assumption). |
| Pains | Waiting in a group chat for flyer versions and feedback loops (Assumption, from the starting idea). |

| Evidence | None specific. |

## Anti-personas

- **Solo or DJ acts.** Excluded from R-001's population; many surveys mix them in [E-02].
- **Acts with a label or manager.** They don't promote themselves [E-02 excludes them by design].
- **Professional promotion agencies and press offices.** They sell the job we replace; they are competitors and a signal, not users.

## Segments to test (not personas yet)

Evidence so far splits only by age (younger and older artists both find self-promotion uncomfortable [E-07]). Candidate splits to test in interviews: gigging live band versus studio-first act; city versus province; one Driver versus shared promotion work; Italy versus UK.

## Evidence audit (persona-validation part B)

Run as a separate self-review pass against the R-001 and R-002 reports.

1. **Every trait is tagged** with an evidence id or (Assumption): yes.
2. **Core backed by medium or high evidence:** **no.** Every evidence-backed trait is Low confidence, so both personas are marked **speculative**. The Driver's main pain (time spent on promotion) has an academic source (E-09) but from other countries; its current workaround has none.
3. **No contradictions:** none found. Note: E-04 ranks money above promotion as the main barrier, and the Driver's pains need to be read with that in mind.
4. **Quotes:** both are labelled illustrative; none is presented as real.
5. **Illustrative details labelled:** names, ages and bands are labelled illustrative.
6. **Anti-personas:** three listed.
7. **Assumptions listed:** yes, with tests.

**Share of traits backed by evidence:** 6 of 19 Driver and Reviewer traits (about one third), all Low confidence. **Both personas are speculative.** They are fit for guiding interview questions and early prototype choices, not for pricing or go-to-market claims.
