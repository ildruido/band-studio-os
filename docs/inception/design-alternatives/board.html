Band Studio OS · design exploration · not a prototype

Three ways the whole product could look and feel

Each alternative covers the full path, from the first open to a band that has used the app for a year, including features past the MVP. They differ in what the app is: a wall of posters, a crew member you talk to, or a control desk. Pick one direction before prototype v2 is built.

Status: proposals only. Nothing here has been shown to a band, tried on a real phone, or built. The posters on the phones are drawn by hand in code, so they say nothing about whether real generated artwork can look this good. That stays with the blind output test. Everything tagged "Roadmap: later" or "New idea" is speculative.

The three at a glance

A. Gig Wall

The app is a wall of the band's posters. One decision per screen. Concrete grey, black ink, yellow tape. The app owns no colour.

Best for: the first interviews and the MVP.

B. Tour Manager

The app is a roadie in your group thread. You talk, paste or forward; it answers with cards. Notebook paper, blue ballpoint, red marker.

Best for: how bands already work (group chat).

C. Control Desk

The app is a mixing desk. Identity is faders, a campaign is a countdown timeline, connectors are patch cables. Dark graphite with amber and red lights.

Best for: power users, and the "OS" idea in the name.

Recommendation

Take A, the Gig Wall, as the base for prototype v2 and borrow two ideas.

  • From B: let a band hand over what it already has (an Instagram or Bandcamp link, a forwarded gig message) instead of answering questions. Test it in the first interviews. It could cut onboarding the most.
  • From C: when a band needs a release or a tour, show it as one countdown timeline inside the wall, not a new app.

Why A: it has the lowest build cost, it is easiest to show in driver interviews, and the band's own poster is the first and last thing on every screen. That matches the product principle that the band's identity comes first. The weak point is later growth, when the wall has to hold gigs, releases and tours. The borrowed timeline is the answer to that. If the first interviews show that bands live in the group chat and will not open another app, B is the stronger long-run bet and A's approval link still works inside it.

What is not checked

  • No band or bandmate has seen any of these. Which one feels right is Ale's judgement, not evidence.
  • Not tried on a real phone, in dark theme, or in Italian.
  • The artwork is drawn in code. Whether generated posters can match it is the open question the blind output test answers.
  • Prices, costs and times on the phones are placeholders.
  • Features beyond the MVP are directions, not commitments. Gig night and multi-band are not on the current roadmap.

Alternative A

The Gig Wall

A wall of the band's own posters. One decision per screen. The app has no colour of its own.

Core idea

Everything the app makes is a poster or a sheet pinned to a wall. You never open a settings page: you pick between two things, finish one sentence, or swipe. The wall is also the history, so the home screen gets richer as the band works, with no new menu to learn.

Look and feel

Concrete grey, black ink, yellow tape, photocopy grain. Wide condensed type for names, typewriter text for detail. All colour comes from the band's artwork, so the poster is always the loudest thing on screen.

Strengths, weaknesses, build cost

Where it is strong

  • Fastest first session and lowest cognitive load
  • The band's own artwork is the hero on every screen
  • Cheap to build: a deck, a short form, a link page
  • Already close to the direction Ale accepted for now

Where it is weak

  • Needs a plan for when the wall holds many gigs, releases and tours
  • Power users may want more control than four buttons
  • Less natural for work that is not a poster, such as booking

What it asks of the build

  • Link-based approval pages
  • An asset deck that regenerates one item at a time
  • Later: a timeline view for releases and tours, connectors, a media picker

Alternative B

The Tour Manager

A crew member in the band's group thread who does the boring promotion work and asks for approval.

Core idea

You do not operate the app, you talk to a roadie. It reads what the band already has, asks you to correct it, and answers with cards: gig, poster, vote, reminder. Bandmates are in the same thread, which is how bands already settle things. Threads per project replace menus, and a notebook page holds what the roadie knows about the band.

Look and feel

Lined notebook paper, blue ballpoint, red marker, masking tape. Handwriting for the roadie, a typewriter face for data. Chat bubbles look like notes stuck on a page, to avoid the standard assistant look.

Strengths, weaknesses, build cost

Where it is strong

  • Matches how bands work: a group chat with arguments
  • Onboarding by handing over links and messages, not by answering a form
  • Growth is natural: new thread, new kind of card
  • The roadie can remember what the band vetoes

Where it is weak

  • Chat is the most common AI shape. The look must carry the difference
  • Comparing images in a thread is harder than on a deck
  • Free-form talk makes output and cost less predictable
  • Needs the most careful tone of voice

What it asks of the build

  • A conversation engine with structured cards
  • Link and message reading for onboarding
  • Threads and membership by link
  • Reminders. Later: voice notes, connectors

Alternative C

The Control Desk

A mixing desk for the band's promotion: faders for the look, a timeline for the campaign, cables for publishing.

Core idea

The product is the control room the name promises. Identity is a channel strip with faders and mutes. A campaign is a countdown with tracks for print, feed, stories, mail and later video. Sign-off is a row of lights. Connectors are cables you plug into a patch bay when they exist. Scenes change which tracks you see for a gig, a release or a tour.

Look and feel

Dark graphite, amber and red lights, stencil-like headings, mono labels. The band's colour lights the faders and the poster meter.

Strengths, weaknesses, build cost

Where it is strong

  • The clearest picture of the whole plan: when each asset goes out
  • Scales to releases, tours and video as new tracks and scenes
  • Makes AI cost visible, which helps trust and pricing
  • Feels like a serious tool for repeat use

Where it is weak

  • Heaviest to learn for a time-poor driver who plays a few gigs a year
  • Dense on a phone
  • The pro-audio look may feel like producers more than punk bands
  • Highest build effort

What it asks of the build

  • A timeline model with takes and scenes
  • A rules engine that places clips by date
  • Sign-off links, cost metering from day one
  • Later: connectors as plug-ins
